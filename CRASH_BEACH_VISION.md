# Crash Beach Vision Brief

**Status:** Owner-approved direction, October 23, 2026  
**Purpose:** Product north star for the post-v0.50 world-foundation rebuild

## North Star

Crash Beach is a story-led survival adventure with enough systemic freedom that different players—and repeated playthroughs—produce different journeys.

It combines authored storytelling with emergent survival decisions. It is neither a linear story with superficial survival mechanics nor an endless sandbox without a meaningful destination.

## Intended Player Experience

The player should remember:

- Becoming capable after beginning vulnerable and stranded.
- Transforming an unfamiliar island into a place they understand.
- Discovering locations that feel genuinely hidden.
- Making consequential survival and relationship decisions.
- Developing an unpredictable, personal history with Wren.
- Reaching an authored conclusion through a journey that felt like their own.

## Game Structure

Crash Beach will contain two complementary modes.

### Campaign

An approximately 8–12-hour authored survival adventure with:

- A defined beginning, escalation, climax, and ending.
- One deeply developed island.
- Meaningful exploration order and route choices.
- Camp construction and visible progression.
- An autonomous Wren relationship system.
- Multiple decisions and outcomes.
- Twelve secret locations and twelve unique treasure items.
- Strong replayability without requiring procedural replacement of the authored world.

### Survival Mode

A separate replayable mode emphasizing:

- Longer or open-ended survival.
- Variable resources, weather, wildlife, and events.
- Rearranged eligible treasure locations.
- Systemic camp development.
- Run comparisons and personal survival goals.
- Less dependence on campaign story sequencing.

The campaign will be designed first. Survival mode will reuse proven campaign systems rather than distort campaign design.

## Core Gameplay Loop

1. Prepare at camp.
2. Choose an expedition goal or route.
3. Explore a meaningful location.
4. Gather resources, investigate discoveries, or advance a story thread.
5. Read warnings and decide whether to risk dangerous territory.
6. Return—or adapt when returning is impossible.
7. Improve the camp, equipment, island knowledge, or relationship with Wren.
8. Begin the next expedition with a changed world state.

Survival, exploration, construction, story, and relationships should reinforce this loop rather than compete as disconnected minigames.

## World Structure

The island will combine three structures:

- A partially obscured island map showing discovered geography and travel relationships.
- Several large, continuous explorable regions.
- Handcrafted locations within those regions, connected through believable terrain.

Recommended initial scope:

- One island.
- Six major regions.
- Approximately 24–30 purposeful locations.
- Twelve secret locations.
- Twelve unique treasures.
- Camps, landmarks, and routes recorded only after discovery.

Distant fast travel may unlock between established landmarks, but it must not replace initial exploration.

## Secrets and Treasure

There will be exactly twelve secret locations and twelve associated unique treasures.

Design rules:

- Every secret must involve observation, experimentation, preparation, environmental reasoning, or a meaningful choice.
- Treasures must provide a distinctive benefit, story implication, capability, or combination of these.
- No treasure will be filler currency or a cosmetic duplicate.
- A thorough campaign can uncover all twelve.
- Survival mode can rearrange eligible placements without changing their identities.
- The interface may show `Secrets 0/12`, but undiscovered names, rewards, solutions, and locations remain concealed.
- Their specifics will not be disclosed to the owner before playtesting unless approval becomes necessary.

## Wren

Wren is an autonomous co-survivor.

She will:

- Maintain her own needs, knowledge, equipment, and priorities.
- Travel and act independently.
- Discover locations or resources without Alex.
- Make decisions influenced by trust and previous treatment.
- Cooperate, disagree, compete, withdraw, reconcile, or become a close ally.
- Remain physically grounded in the same terrain rules as the player.
- Never function as a passive follower or reward dispenser.

Her behavior should create stories, not merely unlock dialogue.

## Danger Model

Danger will be approximately 80% deliberate and 20% dynamic.

Deliberate danger includes:

- Clearly recognizable dangerous regions.
- Hunts chosen by the player.
- Authored encounters.
- Risks tied to exploration depth, darkness, weather, and story progress.

Dynamic danger includes:

- Migrating wildlife.
- Weather changing a familiar route.
- Tracks, calls, damaged vegetation, or other warnings.
- Rare roaming threats outside established safe zones.

Landing Beach and established camps remain dependable safe areas during ordinary play. Dynamic threats must be telegraphed and may not appear directly beside the player without warning.

## Visual Direction

Crash Beach will retain an illustrated identity, but its environments require a gameplay-first overhaul.

The normal view should feel like a natural place:

- Walkable ground is visually unmistakable.
- Paths continue convincingly toward exits.
- Dense foliage, water, cliffs, and wreckage clearly communicate obstruction.
- Resources belong to recognizable sources rather than floating markers.
- Landmarks provide orientation.
- Characters stand on appropriate ground and pass behind or in front of scenery correctly.
- Permanent “TRAIL” labels and oversized navigation overlays are removed.

An optional exploration view may temporarily display:

- Discovered place names.
- Known routes.
- Known resource sources.
- Camps and major landmarks.
- Current destination guidance.
- Secret-discovery count.

It cannot reveal undiscovered locations or treasure.

## Technical Art Principle

Artwork, navigation, collision, resources, exits, and character depth must be designed from the same scene plan.

A finished painting cannot be treated as a background and then retrofitted with unrelated collision rectangles and interface markers. Each location needs coordinated layers:

1. Ground and walkable routes.
2. Obstructing terrain.
3. Interactive objects and resources.
4. Character depth and occlusion.
5. Entrances and exits.
6. Optional discovery information.

Existing backgrounds may remain as concept references, but they should not automatically remain as final gameplay maps.

## Scope Boundary

The first target is a polished 8–12-hour campaign, not an immediate 12–18-hour production commitment.

The project may expand only after its central region proves:

- Movement feels natural.
- Routes are readable without permanent overlays.
- Building visibly transforms the world without trapping characters.
- Exploration is rewarding.
- Survival pressure supports rather than interrupts discovery.
- Wren behaves credibly and independently.
- The art and gameplay geometry operate as one system.

## Recommended Next Phase

Create one representative world-foundation prototype containing:

- The safe landing beach.
- A jungle approach.
- An unbuilt camp clearing.
- A freshwater route.
- One dangerous branch.
- One concealed test location.
- Wren independently traveling between two appropriate locations.
- A functioning optional exploration view.

This prototype should replace the current route-overlay approach and establish the production method for every later region. It should be approved before the remaining island is rebuilt.
