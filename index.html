Crash Beach

Prototype v0.40 — choose Solo or Duo, Story or Survival, and Permadeath or Continue, before you begin. Survival is the full challenge: food and water go a little less far, predators grow more frequent and more dangerous, and gathering spots take longer to refill, the longer you last. Story eases all of that up, at the same map and the same choices. In Duo, whether she comes with you into danger is up to her, not you — and Go Hunting (H) lets you seek a fight out instead of waiting for one. A predator encounter now plays out as its own battle scene — Attack, Defend, Item, Flee, or Spare, no timing required — and the first time you run into each kind of predator, it gets its own short cinematic before the fight starts. On top of that steady climb, the weather isn't always calm — watch for storms, predator swarms, and dry spells passing through. Storms now come in five real severity tiers, Squall up through Hurricane, escalating the longer you last — the first time you hit a new tier plays a short cinematic, with every repeat after that just a quick warning. Kills, crafting, gathering, and every day you survive now earn real XP — leveling up sharpens your Attack, eases your hunger and thirst drain, and eventually makes Flee more reliable and puts something tougher out in the treeline. Continue mode adds real saves — autosaved at a new day or a shelter rest, or manually (K) at camp — so death doesn't have to be the end of a run. The music rotates through several melodic patterns per mood, and a new run now opens with a short cinematic (skippable, shown only once) before you land on the beach — meeting Wren for the first time gets one too, and so does the first time you reach every other part of the island.

How to play

Move — WASD / Arrow keys, or drag the left circle.
Fight — no timing or reflexes needed anymore. When a predator closes in, the screen shifts into its own battle scene with a simple menu: Attack (costs 1 spear durability, always lands), Defend (cuts the predator's next hit), Item (use a Bandage/food/tablet from your pack without leaving the fight), Flee (try to break off — chance of success, and a miss gives it a free hit), and Spare (once it's weakened enough, let it go clean — no kill, no drops, but the island notices mercy). Fight it down to nothing instead and it drops meat, bone, and pelt. Your spear has limited durability and can break mid-fight — Attack turns off until you repair it (Item/Flee/Defend still work). Three kinds prowl the beach from the start — a boar, wild dogs, and a big cat — each with its own toughness, bite, and how hard it is to shake off with Flee. A fourth, the alpha wolf — tougher than all three — starts showing up once you've leveled up enough (see Leveling below).
Go Hunting — H or the red button, anywhere a predator could spawn (Crash Beach, Jungle Interior, the Second Island) and you're not already in a fight. Instead of waiting for something to find you, you go looking for it. (Duo only) Bring Wren along and, past a real level of trust, she'll wade into the fight at your side — the more she trusts you, the harder she hits.
Interact — E or the green button. Drinks at a water source, forages coconuts, catches fish, gathers wood or reeds, eats, or rests at your shelter — whichever is nearest and relevant.
Craft — C or the purple button opens the crafting library: browse every known recipe, see what it costs, and make it if you have the goods. Nothing crafts automatically anymore. A kill's spoils go further at the fire: cook meat for real food, lash a bone onto your spear for extra durability, or stitch a pelt into a cloak that cuts your rest cooldown.
Explore — walk through a gap in the treeline to move between most screens: from Crash Beach, inland to Sean's Camp, and a second, separate gap further east into the Jungle Interior; from camp, west to the Freshwater Stream or north up onto the Ridge, which also connects on into the Jungle Interior, looping back down to the beach. Walk back through a gap to return the way you came.
Swim — some places aren't reachable on foot. Get close to the surf along Crash Beach's southern edge and press E to swim out to the wreck at Shipwreck Shallows, then swim again from there to reach the Low-Tide Wreckage Field further out — no gap to walk through, just open water.
Tide — the Low-Tide Wreckage Field has its own clock: the water pulls back and comes in on a real timer, shown in the location tag while you're there. Its salvage is only exposed and gatherable at low tide — check back later if you swim out at the wrong time.
The Raft — a second island is visible to the east of Crash Beach, but the water there is too much to swim. Craft a Raft (a big, one-time cost of wood, rope, and scrap) and press E at the eastern shore to launch it — a permanent way across, from then on. The island itself is real land: its own spring and coconuts, but also its own danger, and a rare timber (ironwood) that feeds a third, final spear upgrade.
Camp — your home base, and a confirmed Safe Zone: a rain-catch for water, a small firewood stockpile, and a lean-to shelter you can rest at (E) to heal, on a cooldown. No predator can ever spawn here — a good place to practice moving around and crafting without that risk. (Thirst and hunger still drain everywhere, camp included — bring food and manage them like normal.)
Stream — also a Safe Zone: flowing water, a reed patch to gather (rope material), and a fishing spot — fish restore more hunger than coconuts. Keep heading upstream (west) and you'll pick up a second trail.
Solo or Duo — chosen once, at the very start of a run, on the name panel. Duo is the full story below, Wren included. Solo is just you — no one else washed up this run, no meeting, no trust, no camp to merge.
Wren (Duo only) — someone else is out here. Walk up to her at her camp and you'll get one shot at a first impression — approach openly, stay cautious, or hold back — and it sets where Trust starts. Talk to her (E, on a cooldown) any time after that to build it further — high enough trust means real sharing, not just conversation. Get it high enough and she'll move in with you at Sean's Camp for good. Trust isn't one-directional, and moving in together doesn't freeze it in place — leave it neglected too long (even after she's moved in) and it drifts down on its own. Let it fall far enough and she turns cold: she'll avoid you, take from your stores when you're not looking, and any conversation is a guarded one — until trust climbs back out of it. Push either extreme far enough and something bigger happens: keep trust near its peak after she's moved in and the relationship deepens for good, with real day-to-day benefits; let it bottom out completely and she leaves for good this run, taking her things back to her old camp. She's no longer just along for the ride, either — whether she follows you into somewhere dangerous is her own call, more likely the more she trusts you, and she draws real food and water from your shared stockpile rather than living on nothing. She can get hurt out there. She won't die from it, but a bad enough scrape sends her running for home to recover, and she won't be at your side again until she's actually made it back.
Survive — keep Health, Thirst, and Hunger above zero. There's no rescue ending — the whole game is how long you can last. In Permadeath (the default), death is permanent — a new run starts from scratch. In Continue mode, chosen on the name panel, death instead offers a way back to your last save.
Continue mode & saving — an alternative to Permadeath, picked once on the name panel under "On Death." Your run autosaves automatically at the start of a new day and whenever you rest at your shelter, and you can manually save any time with K (or the on-screen SAVE button) while standing at Sean's Camp. Die in Continue mode and the death screen offers Continue from Last Save alongside Try Again — pick it up right where your last save left off. A save doesn't capture absolutely everything (the tide, any weather passing through, and exactly where things stood mid-encounter reset fresh) but your character, inventory, day count, and relationship with Wren all carry over exactly.
The Long Haul — the island doesn't stay static. The longer a run goes, the less any single food or limited water source (canteen, purification tablets) restores — drinking straight from a spring or stream always stays a full restore, so you're never truly stuck, but cooking your kills matters more and more (it's the best hunger restore in the game). Predators also spawn more often and lean more dangerous as the days add up, and gathering spots take longer to refill. Applies the same way in Solo and Duo.
Leveling — a predator kill, a full day survived, crafting, and gathering all earn real Player XP (shown top-left, next to your other stats). Leveling up hits harder with Attack, eases your hunger/thirst drain a little, and at level 3 makes Flee noticeably more reliable. At level 5, the alpha wolf — bigger, tougher, and harder to shake off than anything else out here — starts showing up in the mix, and every predator you meet from then on hits and holds up a little harder too, not just the wolf. Crafting and gathering separately feed a second, Crafting Level (shown in your inventory list): as it climbs, the effective heal, thirst, and hunger restored by anything you craft or repair — Bandages, Hone Spear, Purification Tablets, Cooked Meat — quietly improves. Both tracks reset with every new run.
Surge Events — every so often, on top of that steady climb, something real moves through: a storm, a predator swarm, or a dry spell, each with its own visible warning and its own way of making things harder while it lasts. They pass on their own — the island doesn't stay in one of them forever — but they're worth paying attention to, not waiting out blindly.
The Island — not everything out here has an obvious cause. Long enough, and you might start to wonder.
Tone — set at the start, changeable anytime below. Mild keeps things gentle, Standard is the default, Salty gives the writing real bite. Doesn't touch difficulty, just the words.
